Positioning and launch requirements  
October 14 2026 \| Working draft for design and development

We want a website that helps commerce brands understand how Flieber can improve their inventory planning with software adapted to their operation and optional support from our team. The first version should generate qualified conversations and help us learn what customers value and will pay for.

## Direction for the first version

Present one coherent offer: an inventory planning system built on Flieber’s data, integrations, and planning logic, with flexibility in how the customer builds and operates it. Customers can tailor the experience with our help or their own resources. Those who want continued assistance can engage our team to work alongside theirs.

## Basis and status of this brief

This brief translates the shared voice conversation from October 14 into website guidance. It is not a finalized product specification or commercial commitment. Directions expressed by Fabricio are distinguished below from recommendations developed for the website. Proposed copy, page structure, technical requirements, and launch criteria are recommendations.

## Directions expressed in the conversation

- AI creates two opportunities: customizing software experiences at scale and delivering services with much more automated execution.

- Flieber’s underlying platform combines data, integrations, business logic, and use cases so that customer experiences and workflows can be tailored on top of it.

- Customization may include understanding the customer’s business and building software around its needs. Continued operational support is optional.

- The services opportunity includes doing planning work alongside the customer’s team and providing dashboards that make the work visible.

- The current intention is to charge for ongoing use of Flieber’s data platform. A possible \$200–\$300 monthly license was exploratory, not an approved price.

- The immediate priority is to offer something useful, capture value, and learn through customer response rather than resolve the long-term business model first.

# Audience and positioning

## Primary audience for the initial website

Recommended focus: commerce brands whose inventory complexity has outgrown the capacity of their current team. The buyer may be a founder, operations leader, or planning leader. Their team understands the business but spends too much time reconciling information, maintaining spreadsheets, and reacting to inventory problems.

Fabricio considered software customization a potential SMB route and services a potential move upmarket. Treat this as a commercial hypothesis. The website should let visitors identify their preferred level of help without assuming company size determines their needs.

## Two additional buying situations

- A brand wants to build its own planning tools using AI or internal technical resources, while relying on Flieber’s underlying data and planning capabilities.

- A smaller brand has no dedicated planner and wants help relieving someone who currently handles planning alongside other responsibilities. Price sensitivity and education needs may be greater here.

## Core positioning

Recommended positioning: Flieber helps brands create an inventory planning system that works the way their business operates, backed by an established planning foundation and specialists who can help build and run it.

## Customer outcomes to emphasize

- Greater confidence in replenishment decisions and a clearer view of future inventory needs.

- Less time maintaining spreadsheets and assembling data for planning.

- Better visibility into stockout risk and cash tied up in excess inventory.

- More planning capacity for the existing team, with workflows and views that fit its needs.

Describe these as intended benefits. Publish quantified improvements only when supported by customer evidence.

## Role of AI and experience

Explain AI through what it enables: tailoring workflows, reducing repetitive work, and making support more efficient. Keep technical terms such as vibe coding secondary. The first screen must make sense to a buyer who has little familiarity with AI.

The conversation cited experience with more than 1,000 brands. Verify the count and publishable wording before using it. Experience can support credibility; it does not establish access to customer benchmarks or prove a specific performance gain.

# Offer and commercial presentation

## One foundation with flexible delivery

The following is a recommended way to explain the offer. It reflects the discussion but should be checked against what the team can deliver today.

## Use the Flieber foundation

Flieber supplies the underlying data connections and inventory planning logic. The intended recurring platform license continues when the customer operates its own tailored system without additional Flieber services. Define what the license includes before describing it publicly.

## Tailor the system to your operation

Flieber can work with the customer to understand its processes and develop the views, workflows, and tools it needs. Customers with their own resources may also want to build on the foundation. The supported development method, access model, and maintenance responsibilities remain to be defined.

## Add ongoing planning support

Customers can choose continued assistance from Flieber’s team with day-to-day planning work. This is a separate, optional engagement. State the agreed scope clearly; avoid implying that Flieber automatically owns purchasing decisions or executes every supply-chain task.

## Recommended boundary for customization

Keep shared integrations and planning capabilities reusable, while tailoring the customer experience and workflows. This was an assistant recommendation in the discussion, not a settled architecture decision. Confirm the boundary before promising broad customization.

## Pricing on the initial website

Use a conversation-led commercial approach until packaging is validated. Explain that platform access, customization work, and optional continued support may have different charges. Do not publish the exploratory \$200–\$300 figure, fixed project fees, service packages, or minimum commitments as established pricing.

## Questions the website must answer

- What can Flieber help me do, and what will my team still handle?

- Can I use the system without ongoing operational services?

- What can be tailored, and how do we agree on scope?

- If my own team builds or operates the tools, what do I continue paying Flieber for?

Do not advertise the ability to bring a customer’s own data platform. Fabricio raised it as a possibility and left it unresolved. Likewise, avoid claims of unlimited customization, autonomous planning, guaranteed outcomes, or delivery on day one without confirmation.

# Homepage structure and proposed copy

Recommended first release: one focused homepage with anchor navigation, a contact or booking flow, and required privacy information. Add deeper pages when real customer questions justify them. The following copy is a starting point for review.

## 1 Hero

Proposed headline: Inventory planning built around your business

Proposed subhead: Build the views and workflows your team needs on Flieber’s data and planning foundation. Get help tailoring your system, and add ongoing planning support when you need it.

Primary action: Talk about your planning needs. Secondary action: See how it works. Use the same primary action throughout the page.

## 2 Recognizable planning problems

Show a few concrete situations: your team keeps rebuilding spreadsheets; information is scattered across systems; standard software does not fit how you plan; daily firefighting leaves little time to improve the process. Frame the team as knowledgeable and stretched for capacity.

## 3 What sits underneath your system

Explain the foundation in plain language: connected data, forecasting and replenishment logic, and the business context needed to make planning useful. Pair the explanation with an annotated, verified product view. Only name integrations and capabilities that are available.

## 4 Choose how much help you need

Present the options together: tailor with our specialists; build with your own resources where supported; add ongoing planning help. Make clear that continued services are optional. A ready-made starting experience can be added if its availability is confirmed.

## 5 Concrete examples

Illustrative examples to validate: a replenishment view organized around the team’s purchasing routine; a dashboard highlighting products that need attention; a workflow that combines planning information and customer-specific rules. Show what changes for the user. Label mockups and concepts clearly.

## 6 How an engagement works

Recommended sequence: discuss the planning problem; define scope and responsibilities; configure or build the agreed system; decide whether ongoing support is needed. Confirm the actual process before publication. Do not invent implementation timelines.

## 7 Evidence and final action

Use approved customer stories, product examples, and verified experience claims. Close with: Tell us how you plan today and what you want to improve. Repeat the primary action and explain what happens after submitting the form.

# Design and development requirements

## Visual direction

Use Flieber’s existing approved brand assets with clear typography, generous spacing, and readable product imagery. Prioritize specific planning examples over abstract AI graphics. Make the offer understandable on mobile without relying on animation or hover interactions.

## Navigation and content management

- Suggested navigation: How it works, Your options, Examples, and Talk to us.

- Make headline, supporting copy, offer descriptions, proof, and calls to action easy to edit without rebuilding the site.

- Keep existing customer sign-in accessible if this replaces the current marketing website. Confirm the destination and any URLs that need redirects.

## Lead capture

Recommended fields: name, work email, company, and a short description of the planning problem. Add an optional choice for tailoring a system, building with the customer’s own team, ongoing support, or unsure. Keep qualification lightweight.

Provide visible validation, a useful confirmation state, and a verified delivery destination. If a scheduling tool is used, confirm the calendar, availability rules, and ownership. Do not request account credentials or customer operational datasets through the marketing form.

## Technical implementation

- Build responsive layouts and test keyboard navigation, focus states, contrast, form labels, and text alternatives. Target WCAG 2.2 AA as a development requirement.

- Optimize images and page loading. Use semantic headings, descriptive metadata, and indexable primary content.

- Add spam protection, secure form handling, appropriate privacy disclosures, and consent handling for the selected analytics setup.

- Track primary CTA clicks, form starts, successful submissions, scheduling completions where available, and selected service interest. Keep event definitions consistent.

- Test links and submissions on desktop and mobile, including error states. Confirm that successful leads reach the responsible person or system.

## Scope for the first release

Prioritize the core message, one complete conversion path, evidence the company can substantiate, and easy copy iteration. Defer a customer builder, authenticated portal, public API documentation, pricing calculator, and elaborate resource center until the offer and corresponding capabilities warrant them.

# Launch decisions and acceptance criteria

## Resolve before public launch

- What exactly is available today, including a standard starting system, customization methods, integrations, and customer development access?

- Which audience and initial offer will we test first? Who delivers it, and how much capacity is available?

- What does the recurring license include? How are project work and continued support scoped and priced?

- Who owns tailored code, who maintains it, and what happens when the customer stops using the platform or optional services?

- Which proof, screenshots, customer names, and testimonials are approved for use?

- Where do leads go, who responds, and what will the customer receive after the first conversation?

## Recommended launch experiment

Start with a narrowly scoped offer for one customer profile, such as an existing planning team that needs a tailored replenishment workflow. Use the website to recruit conversations, agree on a bounded initial engagement, and observe which part of the offer customers value enough to pay for.

Track the mix of demand for software, customization, and ongoing support, along with objections and delivery effort. Compare qualified conversations, accepted scopes, and paid engagements. Set numerical targets after confirming traffic expectations and team capacity.

## Acceptance criteria

- A visitor can identify what Flieber offers, whom it helps, and the next step from the first screen.

- The page clearly explains the foundation, customization, and optional operational support without presenting them as mandatory bundles.

- Every public feature, integration, price, and evidence claim has been confirmed by the team.

- The design shows a concrete planning experience, with concepts distinguished from available functionality.

- The primary conversion flow works on mobile and desktop; delivery and analytics have been tested.

- The team can revise the messaging as customer feedback arrives.
