# Flieber Positioning: Website & Video Brief

Nov 27, 2026 · @Fabricio Miranda

## How to use this brief

This is the single source for Flieber's positioning on the website and in videos. It replaces the October messaging. Where this brief and older material disagree, this brief wins.

- **Website agent:** use sections 1 to 8. Section 8 is the homepage structure; sections 1 to 7 are the rules every page follows.
- **Video agent:** use sections 1 to 7 and section 9. Videos carry only the stable story: the problem, the team plus the tool, and the moments brands bring us in.
- **Settled:** everything in sections 1 to 9. **Not settled:** section 10. Do not fill those gaps with your own claims.

No em dashes in any copy.

## 1. Positioning

**One line:** Flieber is a planning system built around your operation, run with a team that works alongside yours.

**Full statement:**

> Commerce brands make purchasing, pricing, advertising and cash-flow decisions every day, and inventory shapes every one of them. The people who make those decisions are buried in the work around them: gathering and fixing data, rebuilding spreadsheets, chasing suppliers. What they know is spread across many people, and nobody has time to step back and ask what AI could take off their plate.
>
> Flieber builds a planning system around how your business actually runs, and operates it with a team that works alongside yours. Your people keep every decision. Each month, more of the work around those decisions gets automated, because our team sees it up close and builds what it learns into your system.

**What changed from October:** the team is no longer a separate service next to the software. The team is how the system gets built around each brand and how it keeps getting better. We lead with the outcome and the people, and the tool is the means.

## 2. Who we speak to

The homepage speaks first to the executive at a multichannel brand. A second path speaks to the person who runs planning alone and is stretched. Both come from how our first three customers bought (ONO Roller, Freshcut Paper, Storelli).

|  | Primary: the executive | Secondary: the stretched planner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who | CEO, COO or founder of a multichannel brand | The one person who plans and places orders, often alongside other jobs |
| What they see | Finance, marketing and operations working from different pictures; a team that is busy but not getting ahead; pressure to "do something with AI" | Late POs, messy channel data, no time to check the forecast before money goes out |
| What they want | A business that runs on AI without rebuilding the team | A second set of hands and eyes that knows this work |
| What they fear | Losing control; paying for something the team already does | Being replaced; losing control of their decisions |
| What we say | Your team keeps the decisions. We bring the system and the people who make it work. | You keep every decision. We take the work you never get to. |

**Who we are not for:** teams that want to own and build their own tools. Point them to Flieber Self-Serve (section 3).

## 3. The offer and how we name it

Flieber is one product. By default, "Flieber" means the system built around your operation plus the team that runs it with you. The only other path is Flieber Self-Serve.

|  | Flieber | Flieber Self-Serve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| For | Brands that want the outcome, not another tool to learn | Teams that want to run the tool themselves or build their own on our data |
| What they get | A planning system tailored to their operation, plus a Flieber team working alongside theirs | The Flieber app, CLI and API, and the data layer on its own |
| Where it sits on the site | Homepage and main path | A secondary path, linked from the homepage and navigation |

**Naming rules:**

- Never use "Managed Services" on the website or in videos. It sounds like outsourcing and triggers "I already have a team."
- Describe the team inside the copy, never as a product name: "run with a team that works alongside yours," "your Flieber team."
- Do not name plans or levels (Lite, Core, Full) in public material. Those names live only in proposals and contracts, where scope and billing need exact terms.
- Do not name the old products (Flieber Inventory, Flieber Studio).

## 4. Core promise and pillars

**Core promise:** Bring AI into your operation without rebuilding your team.

Every page and video supports that promise through four pillars:

| Pillar | What it means | Proof point to use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Built around how you run** | Each brand gets its own system, tailored to its channels, suppliers and way of planning. What it needs becomes part of its system, not a spreadsheet workaround or a wait for a roadmap. | Your logo, your views, your use cases. Tailored, not one-size-fits-all. |
| **A team that sees the whole picture** | What your company knows is spread across many people. Our team's experience spans planning, operations and technology, so it connects what your team knows and spots what can be automated. | Experience from more than 1,000 brands since 2019. |
| **Better every month** | Every recurring task our team handles is turned into an automation or an alert. Context is captured once and kept, so it is not lost when people change. | More of the work around each decision is automated every month. |
| **Your decisions stay yours** | Your team approves every plan and every order. We work alongside your people, never over them. | Nothing moves without your approval. |

**Beyond inventory, in one line:** inventory shapes your advertising, promotions and cash flow, so planning connects finance, marketing and operations. Use this line once per page at most. Do not promise modules for finance or marketing.

## 5. When brands bring us in

Brands rarely buy in a quiet month. They buy when something changes and their current setup stops being enough. The site gets one section built on these moments, and videos can open with any of them.

| Moment | How the prospect describes it | What we say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A planner leaves or changes roles | "The person who knew how all this works is gone." | What they knew gets built into your system, so it stays when people change. |
| A new channel, retailer or wholesale account | "We just added Amazon, TikTok Shop, a big retailer." | We plan every channel from one picture, including wholesale and launches with no sales history. |
| A system dies or changes | "Our planning tool shut down." "We're moving ERP or warehouse." | We set up around your new systems and keep planning running through the switch. |
| Fast growth | "We're aiming to double, and the spreadsheets won't hold." | Planning keeps up with your growth, and your team stays focused on the decisions. |
| An inventory scare | "We ran out of our bestseller." "We're sitting on months of stock." | A second set of eyes on every plan before money goes out. |
| A push to use AI | "The board asked what we're doing with AI." | A practical way to bring AI into operations, run by people who know this work. |

- Any claim that revenue doubled, or any growth figure about Flieber itself
- Any promised outcome, KPI or guarantee for a customer

## 7. Language rules

| Say | Never say | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "A team that works alongside yours" | "We take over your planning," "we own your supply chain" | Brands do not want to lose control. |
| "Your team keeps every decision" | "Replace your planner," "fewer people," "cut headcount" | Headcount talk makes us a rival to the team the buyer hired. |
| "Tailored to how you run" | "Built for \<brand>," "custom software," "we build whatever you need" | We are not a software house. |
| "Your Flieber team" | "Managed Services," "outsourcing," "consulting" | Outsourcing triggers "I already have a team." |
| "Bring AI into your operation" | "AI replaces your team," "fully autonomous" | AI supports the people; it does not run the brand alone. |
| "Automate the work around each decision" | "Our specialists clean your data by hand" | We automate first, and people handle the exceptions. |
| "Experience across 1,000+ brands" | "All we do is forecasting" | Breadth is the proof; narrowness reads as a weakness. |
| "Inventory shapes your ads, promotions and cash flow" | "Flieber only makes inventory decisions" | Flieber sits at the center of the business. |
| "A planning system" | "A full ERP," "a system of record" | Flieber is a system of decision, not a source of truth. |

**Tone:** plain, confident, specific. Write for a busy executive: short sentences, no jargon, no hype words such as "revolutionary" or "game-changing." Never name competitors.

## 8. Homepage structure

The homepage tells one story from top to bottom: the problem, how we solve it, why it works, and the next step. Replace the current homepage and hero now. Keep the Self-Serve and data-layer pages, moved off the main path.

1. **Hero**
   - H1 (recommended): "Bring AI into your operation. Keep your team."
   - Alternatives to test: "Your planning, run on AI. Your team still in charge." / "AI for your operation, with the people who know how to run it."
   - Subhead: "Flieber builds a planning system around how your brand runs, and operates it with a team that works alongside yours."
   - Primary button: "Book a review of your operation." Secondary link: "Prefer to run it yourself? See Flieber Self-Serve."
   - Hero video beside the H1 (section 9).
2. **The problem.** Three short points: your team is buried in the work around decisions; what your company knows is spread across many people; nobody has time to work out what AI could take off their plate.
3. **How it works**, in three steps:
   1. We learn how you run: we connect your data, join your planning meetings and capture the context behind your numbers. Setup takes about the first month, and we add value before it ends.
   2. We tailor your system: your channels, suppliers, rules and views, in a planning system tailored to how you run.
   3. We run it with you and keep improving: your team approves every plan, and every recurring task we handle becomes an automation or an alert.
4. **The four pillars** (section 4), one short block each.
5. **When brands bring us in** (section 5): the six moments as short cards, each with the prospect's words and our answer.
6. **Beyond inventory:** the one line from section 4.
7. **Proof strip** (section 6): 1,000+ brands since 2019, 100+ brands today, the Nixtla result, integration logos. Customer logos only once approved.
8. **Flieber Self-Serve**, one short block: "Want to run Flieber yourself, or build your own tools on our data? Use the app, CLI and API directly." Link to the Self-Serve page.
9. **FAQ**
   - *"I already have a great team."* Good: we work with them, not instead of them. We take the work they never get to and turn it into automations, so they spend their time on decisions.
   - *"Do I lose control?"* No. Your team approves every plan and every order.
   - *"Why does it cost more than software?"* Because it is more than software: a system tailored to your operation and a team running it with you. Inventory shapes your most important decisions, and one avoided stockout or overstock on a bestseller can pay for a year.
   - *"What happens in the first month?"* We connect your data, set up your system and learn how you plan. You see value before the month ends.
   - *"Can I just use the tool?"* Yes. Flieber Self-Serve gives you the app, CLI and API.
10. **Final call to action:** "See what your operation looks like with Flieber." Button: "Book a review."

## 9. Hero video

The hero video runs about 60 to 75 seconds and tells the problem, the answer and how the work keeps improving. It sits next to the H1 and must make sense with the sound off.

| Beat | Time | What we see | Voiceover (draft) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. The problem | 0 to 15 s | A planning team buried in spreadsheets, supplier emails and channel dashboards. Calendars full. | "Every day your team makes decisions that move your whole business: what to buy, where to send it, when to promote. Most of their time goes to the work around those decisions." |
| 2. The gap | 15 to 25 s | The same company seen as separate islands: finance, marketing, operations, each with its own numbers. | "What your company knows is spread across many people. And nobody has time to ask what AI could take off their plate." |
| 3. The answer | 25 to 45 s | A Flieber team member joining the brand's planning meeting. A system with the brand's own logo and views. | "Flieber builds a planning system around how your brand runs, and operates it with a team that works alongside yours." |
| 4. Better every month | 45 to 60 s | A task done once by hand, then shown again as an automatic alert. A plan waiting for the brand's approval. | "Your team approves every plan. Every recurring task we handle becomes an automation, so each month there is less work around each decision." |
| 5. Close | 60 to 75 s | Logo, the one-line positioning, the button. | "Bring AI into your operation. Keep your team." |

**Rules for every video:**

- Show people and outcomes more than screens. The interface will keep changing, so keep product shots short and generic.
- No prices, plan names (Lite, Core, Full) or "Managed Services."
- No headcount language and no outcome numbers beyond the approved proof in section 6.
- Name no customers until approved. Name no competitors.
- Show the brand's team in control: approving, deciding, never being replaced.
- Short videos for other pages each take one pillar (section 4) or one moment (section 5).
