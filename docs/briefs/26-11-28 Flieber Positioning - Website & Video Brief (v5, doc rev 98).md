# Flieber Positioning: Website & Video Brief
2026-11-27 · @Fabricio Miranda

## How to use this brief
This is the single source for Flieber's positioning on the website and in videos. It replaces the October messaging. Where this brief and older material disagree, this brief wins.

Website agent: use sections 1 to 8. Section 8 is the homepage structure; sections 1 to 7 are the rules every page follows.

Video agent: use sections 1 to 7 and section 9. Videos carry only the stable story: the shift to a new way of planning, how Flieber gets the team there, and the moments brands bring us in.

Settled: everything in sections 1 to 9. Not settled: section 10. Do not fill those gaps with your own claims.
No em dashes in any copy.

## 1. Positioning
Tagline: "Flieber: combining AI and specialists to power your inventory decisions."
Short form: "Flieber: where AI meets inventory experts."
Use the tagline wherever there is room to explain: the homepage, decks, email signatures, the site's meta description. Use the short form where space is tight and context does the rest: the logo lockup, video end cards, social bios, banners. Write both exactly as above, with a lowercase word after the colon.
Full statement:
AI is changing how commerce brands plan. Routine work will run on its own, any context a brand has, from past decisions to cash plans, will feed every plan, and intelligence will build up instead of living in a few people's heads. Inventory sits at the center of it, because purchasing, pricing, advertising and cash flow all depend on it.
Most brands know this shift is coming. Few know how to get from the way they work today to the new way, and building it alone usually stalls. Flieber gets them there: a platform tailored to each brand, combining AI with specialists who've seen how more than 1,000 brands operate. The team keeps every call, and the system gets smarter with every cycle.
What changed from October: the team is no longer a separate service next to the software. The team is how the system gets built around each brand and how it keeps getting better. We lead with the outcome and the people, and the tool is the means.

## 3. The offer and how we name it
Flieber is one offer: a platform tailored to each brand, plus the specialists who work with your team. There is no software-only option on the website or in videos. When it comes up, explain why (homepage section 5): software that has to fit everyone leaves the gaps that made spreadsheets necessary, and the value now comes from shaping the platform around each business, which takes people who know the work.
Naming rules:

Never use "Managed Services" on the website or in videos. It sounds like outsourcing and triggers "I already have a team."

Describe the team inside the copy, never as a product name: "specialists who work with your team," "your Flieber specialists." Never "run by Flieber" or "alongside."

Do not name plans or levels (Lite, Core, Full) in public material. Those names live only in proposals and contracts, where scope and billing need exact terms.

Do not name the old products (Flieber Inventory, Flieber Studio).

## 8. Homepage structure
The homepage has one spine. Each section answers one question in the buyer's head, in this order: Why now? What changes? How do we get there? Why not just buy or build software? Is this for me? Can I trust you? What else should I know? Every line must answer its section's question. No standalone lines, slogans or closing remarks between sections. Replace the current homepage and hero now. Take the Self-Serve and data-layer pages off the site.

Hero

H1: "AI that powers decisions. Humans who shape it around your business." Test against: "AI is changing how brands plan inventory. Flieber gets your team there."

Subhead: "Planning is entering a new era: automations that learn, data and context without limits, intelligence that compounds. Flieber takes your inventory decisions there, with a platform tailored to your brand and specialists who've seen how more than 1,000 brands operate."

Primary button: "Talk to a specialist." Secondary button: "Book a demo."

Nothing below the buttons. Hero video beside the H1 (section 9). Do not repeat the tagline in the hero.

Why now: the gap (three cards)

Planning is changing for good. Work that took a planner days now runs on its own. Forecasts take in everything a business knows, not only its sales history. The brands that move first will plan faster and with fewer surprises than the ones that wait.

Few know how to get there. AI is in every board meeting, conference and newsletter. Your team sees the potential, but between daily firefighting and the next purchase order, nobody has the time or the map to bring it into how your business plans.

In-house AI becomes a second job. It starts as an exciting project. Then new use cases appear, people join and leave, and the infrastructure needs upkeep. Soon your team is fixing bugs in a tool it built instead of growing the brand.

What changes: the new era of planning (three cards, in this order)

Data and context without limits. Not just sales, inventory and purchase orders. Add marketing plans, cash flow, competitive information and the reasons behind past decisions. Flieber's AI takes all of it into account in every forecast and plan, so your decisions reflect how your business really works, not just what your systems record.

Automations that get smarter. Flieber specialists gather context from everyone involved in your business, from finance to marketing to operations, and bring what they've learned from more than 1,000 brands. They turn it into automations that take the routine off your team and improve with every cycle, so your people spend their time growing the brand, not running the process.

Intelligence that compounds. Every exception resolved, every plan approved and every outcome observed feeds back in. Insights build on each other and alerts get sharper, so each season you plan with more confidence than the last, and what your team learns stays even when people change.

How Flieber gets you there (three numbered steps)

We learn how you operate. We connect your data and join your planning and S&OP meetings to capture the context behind the numbers, from finance, marketing and operations. Setup takes about the first month, and you see value before it ends.

We tailor your platform. You get your own Flieber, with your logo and colors, built around how your team works. Forecasting models are fine-tuned to your catalog, and each product and store gets the model that performs best. Views, alerts and workflows follow your needs, use cases and preferences, with dedicated forecasting for launches and wholesale. When a new need comes up, it becomes part of your platform instead of a spreadsheet workaround or a long wait on someone else's roadmap.

We stay, and it keeps getting smarter. Your specialists stay with your account. Each cycle, more of the routine moves onto automations, every plan is reviewed with your team before money goes out, and every month you get a scorecard on the measures you agreed on.

Why software alone isn't enough anymore (one block)

For years, software had to fit every customer the same way. Each brand used a small slice of it and filled the gaps with spreadsheets. AI changed that: a platform can now be tailored to each brand, and the value comes from shaping it around how you work. That takes people who know the work, which is why Flieber now comes with the specialists who shape it, and why we no longer sell the software on its own.

Building it yourself is the other route, and it rarely holds: MIT's study The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025 found that partnering with specialists succeeds about three times as often as building in-house. Cite the source on the page (section 6).

Is this for me: when brands bring us in (section 5): the six moments as cards, each with the prospect's words and our answer.

Can I trust you: proof strip (section 6): 1,000+ brands since 2019, 100+ brands today, the Nixtla result, integration logos. Customer logos only once approved.

What else should I know: FAQ

"I already have a great team." Good, because Flieber works through them, not around them. Your team keeps every decision and every relationship with suppliers and partners. What changes is the work around those decisions: data checks, plan preparation and follow-ups move onto automations, new context reaches them every day, and they get specialists who have seen how more than 1,000 brands solve the same problems. Most teams are not short on talent; they are short on time.

"Why not build this ourselves with AI?" You can, and some brands try. The first version is usually exciting. The hard part comes after: every new use case, team change and data source adds upkeep, until maintaining the tool becomes someone's full-time job. MIT found that partnering with specialists succeeds about three times as often as building in-house. With Flieber you start from what works across more than 1,000 brands, and the upkeep is ours.

"What exactly do your specialists do?" They join your planning and S&OP meetings, gather context from finance, marketing and operations, and make sure your platform has it. They keep your data and setup right as your business changes, review plans with your team before money goes out, and turn every recurring task into an automation. Behind your main contacts is a wider team with experience across planning, operations and technology.

"Do I lose control?" No. Your team approves every plan and every order, and nothing moves without that approval. You also decide how much the specialists take on: some brands start with a second set of eyes on their plans, others hand over the whole planning preparation, and you can change that as trust builds.

"Do your specialists leave after setup?" No. Setup takes about the first month, and the specialists stay with your account after that. Their ongoing work is what makes the automations learn and the intelligence compound.

"How do I know it's working?" Every month you get a scorecard on the measures you agree on with us at the start. You also see the work directly: plans reviewed, exceptions resolved and routine moved onto automations.

"Why does it cost more than software?" Because it is more than software. You get a platform tailored to your brand, automations and context your team could not build or maintain alone, and specialists who work with your team every week. Inventory shapes your advertising, promotions and cash flow, and one avoided stockout or overstock on a bestseller can pay for a year.

"What happens in the first month?" We connect your data, map your products, channels and suppliers, join your planning meetings and set up your platform. You see value before the month ends: cleaner data, the first alerts and a first look at your plans with fresh eyes.

"Can I buy just the software?" Not anymore. Software on its own leaves you with the gaps that made spreadsheets necessary in the first place. The value now comes from a platform shaped around your business, and that takes people who know the work.

Final call to action: "See what your planning looks like in the new era." Buttons: "Talk to a specialist" and "Book a demo."
Layout rules for card rows (every row of cards on the site):

Titles in the same row have the same number of lines. Write titles of similar length (about 25 to 32 characters for three cards in a row), and if one must wrap, reserve the same title height for all.

Body text starts at the same height in every card of a row and is top-aligned. Never vertically center card content.

Body texts in the same row stay within about 20% of each other in length.

Cards in a row are the same height, set by the tallest card. Buttons or links, if any, sit at the same height at the bottom.

Check every row at desktop and tablet widths before publishing.
