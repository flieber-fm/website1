Four independent capabilities

Take what you need. Leave what you don’t.

Each layer works on its own. Use one, some or all four, depending on what your team already has.

01 · Data foundation

Consolidated, normalized inventory data

Connectors, consolidated and normalized data, mapping and ongoing ingestion across your sales channels and operational systems.

  • Integration categories: marketplaces, storefronts, ERPs, 3PLs and warehouses
  • Normalization and SKU/product mapping across channels
  • Scheduled refresh with visible data freshness

Use it independently: optional if you already have usable data.

02 · Inventory intelligence

The inventory domain model and logic

Sales and inventory relationships, inventory consumption, projections and replenishment logic, already worked out.

  • Sales vs. true demand, with stockouts and returns accounted for
  • SKU replacements, lead times and open purchase orders
  • Projected inventory and replenishment calculations

Use it independently: works on Flieber data or qualified data you provide.

Bring your own data

Flieber’s data foundation is optional. If your data is already consolidated, bring it straight to the intelligence layer.

  • Warehouse data → Flieber inventory intelligence → your custom UI or a template
  • An external forecast → import it → inventory projection and replenishment
  • No integrated data yet → Flieber data foundation → the capabilities you need

Each path has minimum input requirements. We’ll confirm compatibility with your data during a technical discovery call.

Business logic, worked through

Raw transactions in. Decisions you can trust out.

A simple example: will this SKU make it to its next purchase order?

Inputs
On hand today412 units
Daily demand (stockout-adjusted)18.4 / day
Open PO-1187600 units
PO ETAin 22 days
→
Outputs
Demand until PO arrives−405 units
Projected on hand at arrival7 units
Projected after receipt607 units
Stockout riskHigh: ~0.4 days of cover

Edge case: stockout history

Days with zero stock are excluded from demand history, so past stockouts don’t make the forecast too low.

Edge case: SKU replacements

When a product is replaced, its sales history carries over to the new SKU so the forecast doesn’t start from zero.

Edge case: late suppliers

Lead times come from actual receipt history, not only the quoted number.

Reliability & validation

Check every output before your team acts on it.

Validation shows how well things are working and where they aren’t. It doesn’t make anything “guaranteed correct,” and no tool can.

Reconciliation

Freshness alerts

Missing mappings

Forecast backtesting

Projection error

Questions builders ask

Frequently asked

Our AI can write this. Why use Flieber?

AI is very good at writing interfaces and glue code. It is much less reliable at producing correct inventory logic, such as stockout-adjusted demand, SKU replacements, open-PO timing and lead-time variability, and it won’t keep your integrations running. Flieber gives your builders those parts already working and tested, so the AI-written code sits on something reliable.

Do I have to use Flieber’s data connectors?

No. If your data is already consolidated, for example in a warehouse, you can bring it straight to the inventory intelligence layer, provided it meets the minimum input requirements for the capability you use.

Am I locked in?

The layers are designed to be selected independently, and templates are intended to be open source, so you can use only what you need. We’ll go through data access and portability in detail during technical discovery.

Who owns the integrations and the code we build?

That depends on how you build: self-serve, assisted or Flieber-led. We agree in writing on ownership, hosting and maintenance before any work starts.

Is Flieber really modular?

Yes, with honest limits. Each layer can be used on its own, but some combinations need specific inputs. Replenishment logic, for example, needs inventory state, demand assumptions and supply parameters. We publish requirements for each capability rather than claiming everything works with everything.

Do you have APIs, docs or agent (MCP) access?

Developer documentation, API references and agent-accessible interfaces are being prepared, and we’ll describe them only once they’re released. Talk to us if you’d like early access.
